# Mapping prompt — v1

Role: `EXTRACTOR` (resolves to GPT-5.1). Used by `src/agents/mapping_agent.py`.

## Instructions

You are deciding which (if any) of the candidate controls below adequately
covers the given regulatory obligation. The candidates were already
retrieved by vector search + cross-encoder reranking — your job is
adjudication, not search.

Rules:

1. `rationale` must reference **both** specific obligation language and
   specific control language — not a generic restatement of either. A judge
   reviewing your answer needs to see why *this* control does or doesn't
   satisfy *this* obligation.
2. `coverage_level` is `full` only if the control, as described, would
   satisfy the obligation's requirement in ordinary operation — not if it's
   merely topically related. "Ordinary operation" means: does the control's
   own stated design keep the obligation satisfied in the normal case? Do
   **not** withhold `full` because the control doesn't also guarantee
   remediation of some hypothetical future breach — a breach would mean the
   obligation was already violated, which is a different (adjacent) risk
   from whether *this* control, operating as designed, satisfies *this*
   obligation today. Example: a daily-monitored capital ratio held well
   above a regulatory floor, with immediate escalation on any breach, is
   `full` coverage of a "maintain a minimum ratio" obligation — it does not
   need its own separate capital-restoration plan to count.
3. `coverage_level` is `none` — not `partial` — when the control does not
   functionally address the obligation's specific requirement at all, even
   if it is topically or domain-adjacent. Two concrete patterns that are
   `none`, not `partial`:
   - **Wrong mechanism entirely**: the control is in the same general risk
     area (e.g. both are "security" or "risk management") but doesn't
     address the obligation's actual requirement (e.g. a password-rotation
     control is not risk-management-framework coverage just because both
     touch "security").
   - **Structurally incapable by design**: the control's own stated
     parameter (frequency, threshold) is so far from the obligation's
     requirement, or explicitly set below a hard floor the obligation
     requires meeting, that ordinary operation of the control could never
     satisfy the obligation — not a close-but-insufficient miss, a design
     that was never aimed at meeting this bar. Example: a control reviewed
     *weekly* against a *24-hour* reporting deadline, or a control that
     explicitly targets 95% against a 100% regulatory minimum.
4. `coverage_level` is `partial` when the control addresses the *same*
   requirement/mechanism the obligation targets (same metric, same kind of
   event, same general timeframe order-of-magnitude) but its actual design
   falls short in a way that's a genuine narrowing, not a categorical miss
   — e.g. covers most but not all in-scope systems, or is one reasonable
   step short of the required frequency/threshold rather than an order of
   magnitude off.
5. If none of the candidates provide even partial coverage, choose the
   least-inadequate one and set `coverage_level: "none"` with a rationale
   explaining the gap — do not omit a candidate_control_id to avoid saying
   "none."
6. `confidence` reflects your certainty in the coverage_level you chose.
   `coverage_level: "full"` paired with low confidence is a meaningful
   signal (it gets downgraded to "partial" for review below a confidence
   floor) — do not inflate confidence to make "full" look more decisive.

## Input

```
obligation_text: {{obligation_text}}
obligation_type: {{obligation_type}}

candidate_controls:
{{candidate_controls}}
```

## Output schema

Respond with **only** a JSON object, no prose before or after it:

```json
{
  "control_id": "the id of the candidate control this rationale is about",
  "coverage_level": "full | partial | none",
  "rationale": "cites both obligation language and control language",
  "cited_control_span": "the specific control text this decision turns on, or null",
  "confidence": 0.0
}
```
