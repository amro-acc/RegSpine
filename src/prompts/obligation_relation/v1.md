# Obligation relation prompt — v1

Role: `EXTRACTOR` (spec.md §7.1 — this adjudicates a pair of obligations at
obligation-pair-count scale, the same "high-volume, cheap" profile as
`mapping_agent.py`, not a low-volume `REASONER` judgement). Used by
`src/agents/obligation_relation_agent.py` (spec.md §7.2.13/§7.2.14, roadmap
features 13 "cross-regulation intelligence" and 14 "regulatory contradiction
detection" — one comparison call produces both, since they're just two
possible verdicts of the same underlying question: "how do these two
obligations relate?").

## Instructions

You are comparing two regulatory obligations — possibly from different
regulators, possibly from the same one — to decide whether tracking them as
a *related pair* would help a compliance team avoid duplicated remediation
work or a genuine conflict. Most pairs of obligations are unrelated. Do not
invent a relation because both mention a similar general topic (e.g. both
are "about security" or "about reporting") — that is exactly the failure
mode to avoid; the relation must turn on the obligations targeting the
*same specific mechanism, metric, or data element*, not a shared domain.

Rules:

1. `relation_type` is one of:
   - `"unrelated"` — the two obligations address different requirements
     even if they share a general topic. **This is the default** for most
     pairs; only pick one of the options below if there is a specific,
     citable reason not to.
   - `"overlaps"` — both obligations impose a requirement on the *same*
     underlying control, process, or data element (e.g. both require
     incident reporting to *an* authority, just different authorities or
     timeframes) — a bank could design one control that goes a long way
     toward satisfying both, so tracking this saves duplicated remediation
     work.
   - `"conflicts_with"` — satisfying one obligation as literally stated
     would make it harder or impossible to satisfy the other (e.g. one
     mandates retaining a data element for 10 years, the other mandates
     deleting it after 5) — a genuine tension, not just "different rules
     on a similar topic."
   - `"supersedes"` — one obligation is a strictly stricter or later
     version of the *same* requirement (e.g. a tightened deadline on the
     same reporting duty).
   - `"implements"` — one obligation is a specific mechanism that fulfils
     the other's higher-level goal (e.g. a specific ICT risk control
     requirement implements a general "manage operational risk"
     requirement).
2. `rationale` must cite **both** obligations' specific language — not a
   generic restatement of either. A reviewer needs to see exactly which
   words in each obligation drove the verdict.
3. `dimension` names the specific shared axis the relation turns on (e.g.
   "incident reporting deadline", "data retention period") — `null` if
   `relation_type` is `"unrelated"`.
4. `severity` (`"high" | "medium" | "low"`, or `null`) is only meaningful
   for `conflicts_with` — how serious is the tension in practice. Leave
   `null` for every other relation_type.
5. `resolution_hint` is one practical sentence on how a compliance team
   should handle this relation (e.g. "design the control to satisfy the
   stricter of the two deadlines") — `null` for `"unrelated"`.
6. `confidence` reflects your certainty in `relation_type`, including
   `"unrelated"` — a confident "these are unrelated" is still useful
   signal, not a fallback to hedge with low confidence.

## Input

```
obligation_a:
  regulator: {{obligation_a_regulator}}
  text: {{obligation_a_text}}

obligation_b:
  regulator: {{obligation_b_regulator}}
  text: {{obligation_b_text}}
```

## Output schema

Respond with **only** a JSON object, no prose before or after it:

```json
{
  "relation_type": "unrelated | overlaps | conflicts_with | supersedes | implements",
  "dimension": "the specific shared axis, or null",
  "rationale": "cites both obligations' specific language",
  "severity": "high | medium | low, or null (conflicts_with only)",
  "resolution_hint": "one practical sentence, or null for unrelated",
  "confidence": 0.0
}
```
